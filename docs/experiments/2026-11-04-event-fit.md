# The event fit: brightness to find, gated least squares to measure (2026-11-04)

<!-- index

**Question.** Should the event fit become two steps? Brightness, the objective `fit_event` and `detect` maximise, would find the event. Least squares on a track gated around that curve would measure it. The case for it comes from three results:
- the brightness objective has a flat v–d ridge with about fifty near-equal maxima, so a 1.9 % change in c moves its fits 14–45 % ([case-weather](2026-11-04-case-weather.md));
- least squares has one basin and scales exactly with c;
- the blind `ridge()` tracker misses the aircraft's line on these recordings (4–15 Hz RMS, fits 2–60× too close).

The brightness fit does find the right line, so the information is in the spectrogram; only the blind tracker misses it. This is PLAN item 3's "whitened for finding, original for measuring", and Ferguson (1992)'s procedure with our fit as the gate in place of a hand-set band.

**Method, fixed before the run.** For each of the four development events (Oyster Bay 1 and 2, the eagle cam 1 and 2):
- **The gate.** The committed brightness fit's curve, h × `ideal_curve(t, f0, v, d, t_cpa_emit)` at 15 °C, over the committed fit's window, with h = 2 for Oyster Bay event 1 and 1 otherwise. The gate is ±3 STFT bins (±1.46 Hz) around it.
- **The spectrogram.** The original STFT of the case's audio, not the stored, whitened one: Hann, 8192 samples at 4 kHz, every 0.25 s, power in dB.
- **The track.** In each frame, the gate's peak bin with quadratic interpolation in dB. A frame is dropped when that peak is less than 6 dB above the frame's local noise: the median over ±20 bins around the gate's centre, outside ±5 bins.
- **The fit.** `tracks.fit_track` as it is (unweighted), at 15 °C and at the case's station temperature.
- **The searches.** The case on dev with these events, the weather off at 15 °C and on at the station temperature.
- **The surface.** Oyster Bay event 2's least-squares objective over the brightness profile's grid (v 100–170 m/s, d 3–8 km), at 15 °C and 25.6 °C.

**Criteria, fixed by the PM before the run.**
1. On all four events, the 15 °C gated fit's v and d are within twice the Cramér–Rao bound of the truth-side values, and Oyster Bay event 2's surface has one minimum.
   - The truth-side values and bounds are `crb.py`'s development events, the package bound with the c² correlation (provisional).
   - True v and R: Oyster Bay 1, 136.8 m/s and 6,090 m; Oyster Bay 2, 103.5 m/s and 3,638 m; eagle cam 1, 99.4 m/s and 2,536 m; eagle cam 2, 102.8 m/s and 5,986 m.
   - 2σ in v: 1.5, 1.3, 0.3 and 0.6 m/s. 2σ in R: 206, 145, 39 and 118 m.
2. Under the station temperature's change in c, v and d move 2 ± 1 % on all four events.
3. All four searches keep the true pair at rank 1, with margins no worse than the committed 4.9 % (Oyster Bay) and 2.5 % (the eagle cam).

One counted retune is allowed: the gate's width only.

**Expectation, written before the run.**
- **Criterion 2 holds by construction:** `fit_track` doesn't use c until it maps the curve to v and d.
- **Criterion 1 will probably fail on v or d for at least one event.** Its boxes are 0.3–1.5 m/s and 1.5–3.4 %, and model error alone is about three times the bound on Livermore (model-error stage 1).
- **What the gate should change** is that the fits land near the true line, not 2–60× too close as the blind ridge did. Whether they land nearer the truth than the committed brightness fits is the real question. The committed Oyster Bay event 2 sits at 141 m/s, 5.9 km against the truth's 103.5 m/s, 3.6 km.
- **Criterion 3** is most likely to hold on Oyster Bay and least likely on the eagle cam, whose 2.5 % margin moves with any change in its tracks (adsbfetch section 4).

**Decision line.** If the criteria are met, the event fit becomes brightness to find, then gated least squares to measure: one well-conditioned basin that moves correctly with c, which also lets the station temperature be tested fairly. If they aren't, record which step fails: the gate, the peak picking or the fit.

- **Each fit has one optimum,** and at the station temperature all move by 1.82 % (Oyster Bay) and 1.91 % (the eagle cam).
- **Searches:**
  - Oyster Bay keeps rank 1, with margins of 7.1 % at 15 °C and 5.2 % with the weather on, against 4.9 %. Refined error is 112 and 84 m.
  - The eagle cam ranks its true pair 2nd at both: margins 2.1 % and 2.0 %, and its top pair 14.1 km off.
- **Oyster Bay event 2's least-squares surface on its gated track** has 9 local minima at 15 °C and 10 at 25.6 °C, along the same ridge: 142.5 m/s, 6.13 km (RSS 180.7); 140 m/s, 5.75 km (183.9); 145 m/s, 6.38 km (198.5). The best two differ by 2 %.

**Against the criteria.**
1. **Fails.** Two events are 33–76σ from the truth, and Oyster Bay event 1 is 4–5σ off. The surface has 9–10 minima, not one.
2. **Met, by construction.**
3. **Fails.** The eagle cam is rank 2. Oyster Bay passes, with margins of 7.1 % and 5.2 %.

**The retune isn't spent.** The two far events need a different curve altogether, not a nearby line. The truth's v of 103.5 m/s against the fit's 141 m/s on Oyster Bay event 2 is a Doppler ratio f_pre/f_post of 1.9 against 2.4. A gate a few bins wider can't reach that, so the retune would be a blind sweep.

**Outcome: the event fit doesn't become brightness, then gated least squares, on this evidence.**
- **Gated least squares measures what brightness found.** Its track follows the gate's curve to within 1 Hz, and it reproduces the brightness fits within 1–5 %. It is well-conditioned in c, but it can't correct a wrong find.
- **Where the far events go wrong is the find, or the model, not the measurement.** On Oyster Bay event 2 and the eagle cam's event 2, brightness and least squares agree with each other and both disagree with the truth by tens of σ.
- **The v–d ridge is partly the event's own.** Even on a clean gated track, Oyster Bay event 2's least-squares surface has 9–10 minima within a few percent. So these curves separate v from d poorly, and the brightness objective's fifty maxima are that ridge made worse.
- **The next question is which of the two the far events are.** Is the line brightness found the true aircraft's line, with the straight-pass model failing on it (model error, such as a turn)? Or is it another aircraft's line? The test is cheap: compare each gated track with the true flight's heard curve at the known point, computed from ADS-B.
  - A track that follows the trajectory's curve means model error.
  - One that doesn't means a wrong find.

## Is the line the true aircraft's? The tracks against the trajectories (written before the run)

**Question.** For each of the four gated tracks (gate ±3 bins, as above), does the track follow its true flight's heard curve, computed from ADS-B at the known point? If it does, the straight-pass model is what fails on the far events. If it doesn't, the find locked onto another line.

**Method.**
- **The true heard curve.** `score.modelled_curve` of the true flight's 1 Hz segment (`search.build_segments` of the case's traces) at the reference point on the terrain, at 15 °C. The flights are a1dfeb and ace391 for Oyster Bay, a2baea and a336e4 for the eagle cam.
- **The fit to it.** f0 in closed form and the clock free: the audio-to-UTC offset searched over ±120 s around the case's clock at 0.5 s, then refined.
- **The comparison.** That fit's RSS and RMS on the track's own points, beside the straight-pass least-squares fit's RSS on the same points.

**Criterion, fixed by the PM before the run.** Per event, the track's RMS to the true heard curve is within twice its RMS to the gate's curve.

**Decision line.**
- If the trajectory curve fits the tracks better than any straight pass on the far events, then v and d are the wrong intermediate for them. The pipeline should match tracks to trajectories without them, as locate's search already does, and item 2's straight-pass model is retired as the event representation in favour of the gated track itself.
- If the tracks fit neither, the find locked onto another line.

**Expectation.** Oyster Bay event 2 and the eagle cam's event 2 follow their trajectories' curves better than a straight pass does, a1dfeb and a2baea about equally well, and all four meet the criterion. The reason: Oyster Bay already ranks its true pair first with fits 57σ off, which needs the track and the trajectory curve to agree.

The straight pass has four free parameters on the track and the trajectory curve two (f0 and the clock). So a ratio of 1.6–2.0 is a close fit to the trajectory, and 13–68 is no fit at all.

**Outcome: the two far events are wrong finds, not model error.**
- **The near events (a1dfeb, a2baea) follow their true flights' heard curves** to within 0.4–1.0 Hz, with clocks within 6 s. On them the straight pass and the trajectory describe the same line.
- **The far events (Oyster Bay 2, eagle cam 2) don't.** Their tracks are 3.4–5.6 Hz rms from the true flights' curves, 13–68 times the straight pass's residual, with clock offsets that disagree with the case's other event. The line that brightness found, and the gate then held, isn't ace391's or a336e4's.
- **That explains why find and measure agreed and both missed by tens of σ.** They measured another line correctly.
- **The expectation was wrong.** It predicted the far events would follow their trajectories.
- **Decision.** The straight-pass model isn't retired as the event representation on this evidence: where the event's line is the true flight's, the straight pass describes it as well as the trajectory does.
- **The fault is the find.** On two of the four development events, brightness (`fit` in a hand-set window and band for Oyster Bay, `detect` for the eagle cam) locked onto a line that isn't the true flight's. Those cases still rank their true pair first or second because the search scores each candidate flight's own modelled curve against the spectrogram, not the fitted line. That makes the saved fits a weaker part of the benchmark than its ranks suggest.

## Why did the find choose another line? (written before the run)

**Question.** For the two wrong finds (Oyster Bay event 2 and the eagle cam's event 2), is the true flight's line in the spectrogram, and why did the find's objective prefer the other one?

**Method.** In each event's committed window and band (Oyster Bay event 2: 3020–3130 s, 95–195 Hz; the eagle cam's event 2: its `detect` window, 60–300 Hz):
- **The true line.** The true flight's heard curve from ADS-B at the known point, 15 °C (as in the overlay test). Its f0 and the clock are chosen to maximise the find's own objective on the stored, whitened spectrogram's `local_whiten` crop, exactly as `fit_event` scores a curve. That objective is brightness for Oyster Bay (the `fit` command) and the sweep-weighted contrast for the eagle cam (`detect`'s refine step). f0 runs over the band on a 0.25 Hz grid and the clock over ±30 s of the case's clock on 0.5 s.
- **Its presence.** A track gated ±3 bins around that best true curve on the original STFT, as in the gated test. Presence is the share of frames whose in-gate peak is at least 6 dB above the local noise.
- **The scores.** The found line's score (the committed fit's curve) and the best true line's score under the same objective, plus the true line's rank among the find's own grid search's top candidates.
- **What the found line is.** Its f0 and heard CPA time against the true line's best. Its frequency ratio to the true line, to see whether it is a harmonic or a sub-harmonic (ratio near n or 1/n). And which flight in the box its gated track follows best: every flight with a heard CPA within the window ±120 s and within 15 km, with f0 and the clock (±20 s) free, by RMS.

**Criterion, fixed by the PM before the run.** The true line is present at ≥ 6 dB on at least half the frames, and the find's objective scores the found line higher than the best true line. If both hold, the find's objective, not the evidence, chose wrong.

**Decision line.**
- **If the criterion holds,** the find needs a model-consistency term: the trajectory-matched curve as its candidate set, which locate already has.
- **If the true line is absent,** the evidence simply isn't in that window.
