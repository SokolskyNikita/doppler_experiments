# Tier 1: detection and fits against the blind labels, and the known point (2026-11-04)

## Question

PLAN Phase 1 item 5 asks for the one tier the runner lacked: `detect` and `fit` against the labels, plus the known-point
check, on the development data, in under 3 minutes. How do today's detector and fits score against the blind labels
of the two development spans, and does the known point hold?

## Setup

- **Units.** Tier 1 scores labelled spans, not cases (`bench/tier1.json`): Oyster Bay 0–1200 s on its case
  spectrogram, and the eagle cam 3600–4800 s on the full stream's spectrogram, since no case covers that span. ESID
  740's span is listed as calibration and runs only with `bench tier1 --set calibration`, which Phase 4 does once;
  its spectrogram isn't built. The known point runs on oyster-bay, eagle-cam-clip-18000s and
  oyster-bay-hp150-aac32k.
- **What the run may see.** From a label file, only the span and each window's start, end and labelled lines, by
  position. Window ids, classes, notes, associations and trajectory references are truth. They are read only by
  the comparison step, after `span_result.json` and its hash are written; a record that changed after its hash is
  discarded unread, as for Tier 2.
- **The span record.** Every detection `detect` makes on the span (the raw-audio profile's band and window, top
  200, min_score 0, cached by `cache.cached_detections`), and a fit of every labelled line in its window and band
  per objective (brightness, contrast), in a process pool. A rising labelled line gets a missing fit, "rising line,
  no straight-pass model", and never reaches the fitter.
- **The known point.** The events are chosen blind and written with their hash (`events.json`): the case's saved
  fits, and the cheap raw-audio rule, detect on each saved event's window ± 240 s, keeping the best-scoring
  detection whose heard CPA lies inside that window. The whole-recording raw-audio rule stays the raw-audio
  profile's. After the hash, every flight pair is scored at the reference coordinate into `known_point.json`,
  which says in its own `truth_conditioned` field that it is not a blind result, and records the events used and
  the detection cache keys.

**Knobs**, set before any detection was matched against the full spans:

| Knob | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Crop around a span | ± 240 s | detect sees this much context; a detection belongs to the span by its heard CPA, so one whose window straddles the edge is judged on partial evidence |
| Heard-CPA slack | ± 5 s | labels are read to a few seconds |
| Line band | ± 10 % of a line's entry–exit range | for 5–10 Hz reading error |
| Fundamental | f0 × k on exactly one falling line for one k ∈ {½, 1, 2}, no other k landing on that line, and with two or more falling lines 2 f0 k on another | see below |
| Fit band | a line's range ± 10 % | |
| Association agreement | heard CPA within 10 s and f0 within 10 % (at any k) | |
| Counted windows | clear, overlapping | matched or missed; a counted window with only rising lines is "outside the model", neither |
| Not counted | uncertain, masked | nor the detections inside them |
| Budget | warm run under 180 s | PLAN 5.4; the cold time is reported beside it |

**The matching rule was tightened before the full spans were matched.** The first rule accepted f0 × k on any line
for k ∈ {½, 1, 2}. On a two-line window that accepts almost any f0 from 30 to 380 Hz. Under the rule in the table, the
f0 values that can match each committed window (0.5 Hz steps from 60 to 300 Hz, 481 in all) are:

| Window | Lines (Hz) | Matchable f0 |
|---|---|---|
| ob-e03 | 130→65, 345→255 | 115–286 Hz, 118 steps: its lines are two sources, so only k = ½ or 2 matches |
| ob-e05 | 235→165, 205→140 | 25 steps |
| ob-e08 | 235→172, 262→240, 405→385 | 127 steps |
| ob-e10 | 300→170, 250→140 | 107 steps |
| sw-e01 | 165→105, 205→165, 270→240 | 353 steps |
| sw-e07 | 300→255, 185→160, 140→118 | 232 steps |
| e740-e06 | 300→230, 160→115 | 357 steps (calibration; not run) |

The three-line windows stay broad because their bands overlap. The smoke run's two Oyster Bay matches had been read
under the first rule before it was changed.

**A model-error measurement from the development recordings.** For ob-e03 (N360NY, 7.25 km, probable), the curve
modelled from its real trajectory falls fastest at 124.7 s, 15.3 s after its emission CPA (109.4 s) and only 5.8 s
before its heard CPA (130.5 s). A straight pass at that range puts the steepest fall at the emission CPA, 21.1 s
before the heard CPA. So this pass's track is far from straight over the curve (E6). On Livermore, SDE-D found the
model's steepest fall a robust SD of 6 s from its emission CPA ([livermore-audit](2026-11-04-livermore-audit.md));
this one is 15 s, from the development data itself.

## Outcome

Tier 1 exists and meets its budget warm, under load: `bench tier1` scores detection and fits against the blind
labels of the two development spans and checks the known point on three cases in 108 s. The cold run takes 788 s,
mostly detection, which the cache absorbs once per audio and detector version. If the warm budget gets tight, the
window fits, the warm run's whole cost, can be cached the same way. The first numbers are a floor to improve on:
- detection finds 2 of the 6 counted windows, with 9 false alarms per hour on each span;
- no probable association is matched by a consistent detection;
- the labelled-window fits miss their flights by tens of seconds and kilometres;
- the known point holds for Oyster Bay's saved fits only.

## Background spans: detections with nothing to find

**Question.** How many detections does `detect` make per audio hour, against min_score, on recordings with no
propeller flyover? This is the first input to PLAN Phase 2 item 6's threshold curve (5.1 negatives).

**Setup.** Nine development spans, 9.3 h of audio, labelled by construction by `tools/data/background_labels.py`:
- the three jet-only Essex xeno-canto recordings, each one absent window. "Absent" there means no propeller pass, so a
  detection on a jet is the E5 false alarm this curve has to show;
- AeroSonicDB's six soundscape hours: each run of its 5 s "no aircraft" mark lasting at least 60 s is an absent window,
  less a 10 s guard at each end (3.84 h in all); everything else (aircraft audible, the "ignore" boundaries, short quiet
  runs, the guards) is masked.

Their spectrograms were rebuilt from the raw audio by recipe (`derived/background/`, hashes in `bench/tier1.json`, a
rebuild byte-identical).

**A metric change.** False alarms are now counted per hour of the audio that can hold one: the span less its
uncertain, masked and outside-model windows. Before, the whole span was the denominator. Oyster Bay's rate above was
9.0 per hour under the old denominator and is 13.0 under the new; the eagle cam's goes from 9.0 to 17.9. Every rate
below uses the new one.

**Runs** at 6454f67, clean tree, default workers: the first run, cold for the background detection,
`runs/2026-11-04-bench-6454f67-t1/`, took 3,196 s at a load average of 130 (9–332). The warm run,
`runs/2026-11-04-bench-6454f67-t1-2/`, with the window fits now cached too, took **30.5 s** for all eleven development spans
and the six known points, at a load average of 34. Both are driven from `runs/2026-11-04-tier1-background-2/`. A first
attempt (`runs/2026-11-04-tier1-background/`) stopped on four NaN window fits, which fit_event on dev now returns where it
raised before; they are missing fits with that reason now.

**Against the labelled spans.** The matched flyovers score 4.28 on Oyster Bay and 2.46 on the eagle cam. At those
thresholds the background gives 1.8 and 7.0 false alarms per hour; to reach one per hour, min_score has to be about 6,
which keeps neither match. Today's detector can't hold a flyover without a false-alarm rate of several per hour on
background audio, which is what item 6 starts from.
