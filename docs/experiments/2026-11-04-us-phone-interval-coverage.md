# How often does a 5–60 minute U.S. phone recording overlap a tracked propeller plane?

## What counts as a hit

A hit requires positive elapsed time with at least one classified fixed-wing piston aircraft or turboprop within the [modeled acoustic range](2026-11-04-indoor-outdoor-coverage.md) of the recorder. At each candidate duration, recordings tile local time independently: for example, 15-minute recordings start at :00, :15, :30 and :45. Simultaneous or repeated passes in the same bin count once. A flyover spanning two recordings can qualify both. Touching the radius at an isolated instant or ending exactly at the start of the next recording does not qualify that next recording. There is no minimum time in range and no test for a second usable pass. These rates concern opportunities, not the fraction of recordings the geolocator can solve.

| 60 min | 14.18% | 1.27% | 9.84% | 6.96% | 571 million |

On the same four dates, unique occupied seconds accounted for 0.612% of daytime and 0.0179% of overnight time, or 0.413% of phone-available instants. Independent scoring of positions sampled every five minutes with the same residents and activity diaries agreed within 0.001 percentage point on each date and period. A longer window intercepts more real flyovers even if the aircraft is audible for only a small part of that window. Neighboring windows and homes can share the same aircraft.

For one-hour windows, date-specific daytime rates ranged from 13.1% to 15.8%, and overnight rates from 0.82% to 1.68%. These spans describe the four observed dates, not statistical confidence intervals. The native files contained 67,671,803 classified airborne segments in total. The 32,768 sampled residents occupied 30,182 distinct block-group centers. The summary CSV retains all five window lengths on each date.

## Calculation

- The input comprises four complete UTC-day adsb.lol archives: 2024-04-07 (Sunday), 2025-03-10 (Monday), 2025-04-22 (Tuesday), and 2025-09-19 (Friday). We extracted consecutive reported airborne positions for known fixed-wing prop types, preserving per-endpoint height references. We rejected reported gaps over 120 s, new-leg boundaries, grounded fixes and unobserved cross-midnight segments. Position, altitude and motion within each retained segment were interpolated linearly. Missing low-altitude tracks and silent intervals in reception can omit opportunities. The dates are a convenience sample, including the Sunday before the 2024 eclipse. The earlier national estimate used additional monthly hourly snapshots; those snapshots cannot resolve short flyovers between hours. [2024 flight archive](https://github.com/adsblol/globe_history_2024) · [2025 flight archive](https://github.com/adsblol/globe_history_2025)
- We sampled 32,768 residents with replacement, with probability proportional to 2020 Census block-group population across the 50 states and DC. Each resident stays at the population-weighted block-group center. Geoid/terrain, two geographic background-noise groups, aircraft class and flight-phase proxies, and daytime and nighttime house/outdoor thresholds are inherited from the earlier study. For intervals as long as an hour, ignoring geographic travel matters especially for commuting or long trips. [Census population centers](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/time-series/geo/centers-population.html)
- The NHAPS all-age diary distribution was reconstructed from 9,386 individual records. Each simulated resident/day draws a weighted, complete minute-by-minute historical activity diary from the corresponding weekday/weekend stratum, preserving the original adult/child population mix. The draws for the parts of two local dates within a UTC archive are independent. Vehicles and unknown settings have zero modeled opportunity. Other indoor settings use closed-house acoustics. At own/other residences the simulated window state is correlated through the day using one resident draw with thresholds 26.6% open during 06:00–22:00 and 25.9% open overnight. Each represented minute applies closed, open or outdoor acoustic conditions; the ambient-noise model switches at 07:00 and 19:00. [NHAPS data](https://www.timeuse.org/ahtus/data)
- Spatial indexing conservatively finds all home centers near a moving segment. A quadratic line–sphere intersection yields entry/exit times, including both-endpoints-outside crossings. The solver reconciles geometric heights with local geoid and terrain before interpolation. If conditions change during a segment, the intersection is clipped to the minute that uses that radius. Segments and aircraft are unioned before computing unique occupied seconds. We mark five-minute bins first, then take the logical OR across consecutive groups of 2, 3, 6 or 12 of those bins. Every tested duration divides one hour, and the U.S. zones in these archives have integer-hour UTC offsets throughout each sampled UTC day. Thus each resident contributes exactly 960/L day and 480/L night windows per archive, with L in minutes. Sound travel time was omitted consistently with the earlier proximity calculation.
- Conditional percentages concern windows in which a nearby phone is available. Extrapolated counts use the earlier rough 80% ownership, 88% nearby by day and 89% nearby overnight, and 341,784,857 residents. We assume availability holds throughout an individual window and is independent of the nearby plane within each time period. National data do not establish continuous phone proximity for each recording length. The pooled daytime and overnight estimates weight the four dates equally, so the five-minute archive density cannot overweight a particular date. Counts for different recording lengths should not be added. [Population](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/national/asrh/nc-est2025-agesex-res.csv) · [Phone proximity study](https://gtubicomp.pbworks.com/w/file/fetch/46471141/fp537-dey.pdf)

The native archive extraction and scorer jobs are `2026-11-04-us-native-prop-segments-2` and `2026-11-04-us-phone-interval-scores-fixed` (the extraction resumed on another worker).

Synthetic checks covered duplicate passes, a flyover crossing hour and bin boundaries, the 06:00 boundary, different residential UTC offsets, and durations below the five-minute base bin. A separate comparison checked 1,000 randomized geometries against a millisecond-resolution independent distance evaluation. For the real results the aggregation checks every resident's short/long-bin nesting, denominator limits and that hit-window share exceeds the occupied-time share.
