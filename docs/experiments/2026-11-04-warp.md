# Emission-time resampling (de-Dopplerisation)

## Question

PLAN Phase 2 item 8. For each candidate observer, clock and trajectory, resample the recording at
emission time plus propagation delay, so that a steady emitted tone from the right geometry becomes a
stationary line, and score it with short-block harmonic power. Does that separate the truth from wrong
locations better than today's curve score, and better than a least-squares fit of tracked frequencies?

**Criterion**, fixed before running, as PLAN states it: the warp is adopted only if all of these hold
on the development events:
- it ranks the reference first;
- the wrong-box winners reach the reference's strength no more often than under control (1);
- it refines no worse;
- it runs within the Tier 2 budget;
- its gain survives the broader-tone, joint-clock control.

The PM added the consequence: at its cost the warp can't be the search's scorer. If it separates
better than the controls, its place is as a verifier of the few candidates search and refine leave;
if it doesn't, it isn't adopted anywhere.

## Setup

- **Code: `src/dopplertrack/warp.py`** (new).
  - **Reception time.** t_r = u + |p(u) − x|/c, so t = τ + r/c. This is Kelly & Wilson 1993 eq. 12
    and Ramseier & Pieren eq. 9, both checked on the PDF pages: the Markdown copies lose a minus sign
    and mislabel eq. 9's left side.
  - **Positions** are interpolated linearly between the 1 Hz segment samples, as today's scorer does.
  - **Interpolator.** A Hamming-windowed sinc of 32 taps (Ramseier & Pieren eqs. 7–8), after a
    zero-phase low-pass at sr/(2 × 1.35) = 1.48 kHz.
  - **Refusal.** A geometry needing a reading rate above 1.35 is refused.
  - **No amplitude correction.**
- **Output grid** at the 4 kHz input rate. The reviewer's folding example, a receding pass lifting
  440 Hz to 572 Hz on a 1 kHz grid, doesn't bite here: anything folding into the 40–700 Hz scoring
  band would need input above 2.6 kHz. The low-pass guarantees it, and the band-limited pass keeps
  the 4 kHz grid.
- **Score.**
  - The warped signal is cut into 2.048 s Hann blocks at a 0.512 s hop, the package window. So the
    warp's and the window's gains stay separable. The [window record](2026-11-04-window-length.md)
    shows whitened noise has the same spread at every window, and a tone's excess here is that of
    the 2.048 s control.
  - Each block's dB is reduced by its median across frequency within ±10 Hz (Ramseier & Pieren
    eq. 11).
  - Per block and fundamental, harmonics h = 1–3 are pooled with weights (N + 1 − h)/N, each taking
    the best of ±1 bin, over the harmonics inside the scoring grid only. The grid is 40–700 Hz, or
    40–450 Hz on the band-limited pass.
  - A path over blocks follows the fundamental, with an off state and birth and death costs, and up
    to two tone families. The second family is sought after excluding the first's harmonics, and the
    two are summed.
  - Harmonic agreement earns nothing beyond the pooled power: a wrong warp preserves ratios too.
- **Two tone settings.** Strict, one fundamental per event, is the judged setting. Bounded drift is
  reported.
- **Candidates.** The reference is placed at the exact true point (flights a1dfeb and ace391). Ten
  wrong ones are taken greedily from the development search's ranking
  (`docs/results/oyster_bay_search_best_per_pair.csv`), each at least 5 km from the truth and from
  each other, each with its own flight pair.
- **Events.** The README windows: ev1 2090–2200 s at 95–200 Hz, ev2 3020–3130 s at 95–195 Hz. f0 ranges
  are 0.85–1.15 × the saved fits' f0 (112.6 and 132.4 Hz).
- **Clock.** Joint across the two events, ±15 s around each candidate's own clock, searched coarse to
  fine: 0.5 s steps, then ±1 s at 0.1 s, then ±0.2 s at 0.02 s. It is identical for every scorer.
  - The approved coarse step was 0.1 s. The 0.5 s step costs nothing in resolution because every
    score is smooth in T at that scale: a 0.5 s clock error leaves the warped line a residual sweep
    of about 0.5 Hz at CPA, about one bin.
- **Scorers:**
  - (1a) today's curve score (`EventWindow.sweep_scores`) on `local_whiten`, with the README
    harmonics (2 and 1);
  - (1b) the same on the across-frequency background, so the whitener and the warp are separable;
  - (2) least squares of `ridge()` tracks to each trajectory's Doppler factor D, with f0 in closed form
    (f0 = Σ D f / Σ D²) over orders 1–3 inside the f0 range. The score is minus the RMS relative
    residual;
  - (3) the warp.
- **Wrong-box test.** The moved boxes' top candidates from today's search, from the saved-fit bench
  runs: north 150 km (35 candidates) and WSW 150 km (61 candidates). Ten were taken greedily at least
  5 km apart in each box. The windows, clock and f0 freedom are the same as above. It is a
  candidate-set approximation of a whole search.
- **Refinement** (the PM's option a), around each scorer's top candidate, its flight pair fixed. The
  clock is re-searched ±1 s at 0.1 s.
  - Grids: 200 m spacing over ±1 km (121 points) for every scorer; 50 m over ±1 km (1,681 points) for
    the controls.
  - The full 50 m grid was not run for the warp: at the measured cost it is about 14 CPU-hours.
- **Band-limited pass.** All of it again on the audio band-limited to 40–450 Hz (8th-order
  Butterworth, zero-phase), except the refinement.

## Knobs

| Knob | Value | How it was set |
|---|---|---|
| sinc taps | 32 (L = 16) | a priori (Ramseier & Pieren), checked by the Tier 0 floor |
| low-pass, refusal | 1.48 kHz, rate > 1.35 | from the largest |R′|/c (0.29 here) |
| block, hop | 2.048 s, 0.512 s | the package window |
| background | ±10 Hz median across frequency | a priori |
| harmonics, weights | 3, (N + 1 − h)/N | a priori (Ramseier & Pieren) |
| θ (off-state threshold) | 5.82 dB | noise: the 95th percentile of the pooled evidence on white noise warped with the reference's geometry |
| birth = death | 5.82 dB | θ |
| drift step | 0.151 Hz per 0.512 s step | a quarter of the smallest peak sweep rate among the candidates (1.18 Hz/s), as the PM required; 0.31 of a bin |
| drift range | 2 % of the median | a priori |
| families | 2 | as amended |
| candidates | 10 wrong, ≥ 5 km apart | a priori |
| clock, f0 | ±15 s joint; 0.85–1.15 × fit | the same for every scorer |

None of these was tuned on Oyster Bay.

## Numbers

### Tier 0 (`tests/test_warp.py`, 7 tests)

A synthetic 100 m/s pass at 300 m, 440 Hz near the band's top edge, with a peak reading rate of 1.29.
The floor is the line over the strongest other component in 40–1500 Hz, measured with a
Blackman–Harris window:

| | approach | recede |
|---|---|---|
| sinc | 76.0 dB | 74.8 dB |
| linear | 38.0 | 45.0 |
| nearest | 20.6 | 24.4 |

- This is Howell's ordering, with the sinc above 50 dB.
- The low-pass keeps folded noise from 1.6–2 kHz out of the band.
- A rate of 1.47 is refused.
- The true geometry gives a stationary line and a wrong one doesn't.
- The off state and the drift path work.
- `pooled()` ignores harmonics beyond the grid (the regression test, below).

### Ranking, the 11 candidates

z = (s_ref − s_best_wrong) / MAD over the candidates:

| scorer | reference rank | z | raw (ref − best wrong) | best wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1a today's curve score | 1 | +2.41 | +2.62 (28.40 against 25.78) | wrong 2 |
| 1b curve score, across-frequency background | 1 | **+3.33** | +3.22 | wrong 2 |
| 2 least squares on ridge() | 3 | −1.50 | −0.030 | wrong 11 |
| **3 warp, strict** | 1 | **+0.07** | +0.12 (14.74 against 14.62) | wrong 2 |
| 3 warp, drift | 1 | +0.55 | +0.42 | wrong 2 |

- **Band-limited pass, 40–450 Hz:** the same to the third decimal. Warp strict z +0.06, drift +0.52;
  1a, 1b and 2 unchanged. Both events' bands lie under 450 Hz.
- **Drift freedom leaks.** Drift raises the reference's warp score by 2.13 but the wrong ones' by a
  median of 3.14 (up to 4.13). It explains wrong geometry away, which is why strict is the judged
  setting.
- **The warp's event-2 tone.** At the reference its first family sits at 127.7 Hz. That is +1.3 % on
  the f0 event 2's trajectory needs (about 126.1 Hz; the saved fit is 5 % high,
  [event2-bias](2026-11-03-event2-bias.md)). Event 1 sits at 114.3 Hz.

## Outcome

**Not adopted, as the search's scorer or as a verifier.**
- **Criterion, clause by clause:**
  - it ranks the reference first: yes;
  - wrong-box winners reaching the reference: 0 of 2, the same as control (1), which satisfies
    "no more often";
  - it refines worse: 200 m against 0 m on the same 200 m grid;
  - it is outside Tier 2;
  - it has no gain to survive the broader-tone, joint-clock control: its separation of the truth is
    the weakest of the three working scorers (z +0.07 against +2.41 and +3.33). In the wrong boxes it
    also leaves the smallest gap.
- **The consequence.** The warp doesn't separate better than the controls, so it isn't adopted
  anywhere. A verifier must separate better than what it verifies.
- **Why it underperforms here: possible reasons, none tested.** On these events the curve score may
  already resolve the sweep, so undoing the Doppler shift adds little. A wrong geometry's warp still
  leaves the line within a few bins over a 2.048 s block, so it keeps much of its evidence. And the
  off-state threshold, set on noise, may let a wrong geometry's scattered strong blocks score.

Leads, to keep:
- **The across-frequency background improves today's scorer.** (1b) has z +3.33 against +2.41, and
  wider wrong-box gaps (−8.08 and −5.83 against −6.66 and −4.98). That is a whitening change only. It
  is a candidate for Phase 2 item 6 or a scorer change, measured on its own.
- **The warp recovers event 2's rest frequency** to +1.3 % of what its trajectory needs, where the
  saved fit is +5 %. As an f0 estimator for a known geometry it is better than the fit. That would be a
  refinement step, not a locator.
- **Drift freedom absorbs wrong geometry** (the gain on wrong candidates exceeds the gain on the true
  one). Any future warp scorer should stay strict.
- **Least squares on `ridge()` tracks fails.** It ranks the reference third and lets both wrong boxes
  reach the truth's strength. That's a verdict on `ridge()`'s tracks of these events: they wander
  (the [freqtrack](2026-11-04-freqtrack.md) record), so the least-squares comparator needs item 3's
  tracker first.

Limits:
- This is Oyster Bay only, two events. The eagle cam waits on B's event-pairs test.
- Harmonics are pooled at h = 1–3 with f0 searched near the fitted value, so a pass whose fundamental
  lies below the band would be scored on its overtones only. That doesn't arise here; both
  fundamentals lie above 95 Hz.
- Multipath is not modelled.
- The full 50 m warp refinement was not run.
- Runtimes are contended.

## Correction during the work

The first ranking run used a `pooled()` that clipped harmonics beyond the grid to its last bin. That
credited power at the grid's top edge and counted it as observed, which the band-limited pass would
have exposed. The bug was found in review and verified by the PM. The run was stopped before any
number was read and restarted after the fix: `pooled()` now masks harmonics off the grid or under
excluded cells and normalises by the observable weights, with a regression test. The stopped run's
log is `warp_full_stopped_pooled_bug.log`.
