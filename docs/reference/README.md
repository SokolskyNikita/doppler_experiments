# Reference documents

Papers, books, and video references behind the technical choices in [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md). Collected 2026-11-03.

The files themselves live outside the repo, in `$DOPPLERTRACK_DATA/reference/` (by default `~/data/dopplertrack/reference/`). This README and the conversion scripts in `tools/reference/` are tracked.

- `pdf/`: the originals. These are the source of truth, especially for equations, figures and tables.
- `md/`: Markdown conversions for fast searching and reading. Each page starts with a marker like `{149}------`, the **0-based** PDF page index: `{149}` is page 150 of the PDF (`pdftoppm -f 150 -l 150`). Printed book page numbers differ: in Kay I, book page = PDF page − 8; in Kay II, book page = PDF page − 13. Figures sit next to each file in `<name>_images/`.
- `youtube/<video-id>/`: downloaded video, annotated Markdown transcript, subtitles, captions, ASR/OCR output, visual evidence, provenance, validation report, and artifact checksums.
- `youtube/tooling/`: acquisition/review scripts and configuration; logs and temporary previews live under `work/<video-id>/`. The local `youtube/README.md` indexes the videos and `youtube/tooling/README.md` explains rebuilding them.

The material is copyrighted and the upstream repo is public, so it stays on this machine and never goes into git.

Open-source projects on the same problem, the commits inspected and what PLAN.md takes from them: [related-projects.md](related-projects.md). Every search for data, what it found and why each source was taken, deferred or rejected: [datasets.md](datasets.md). Public course materials mapped to the method's steps:
[course-materials.md](course-materials.md); what they say about our failures: [course-reading-review.md](course-reading-review.md)
(2026-11-04; its "PLAN phases" refer to the plan of that date).

## How to use this folder

1. Search the Markdown first: `rg -n "Cramer-Rao|CRLB" "$DOPPLERTRACK_DATA/reference/md/"`.
2. Before relying on an equation, a number or a figure, open that page of the PDF. No conversion is perfect (see [Conversion](#conversion)).
3. Cite by author, year and equation or page, e.g. "Ferguson & Quinn 1994, eq. 15".

**Read first for the next development cycle:** Ferguson & Lo 2000 §I (the model, eqs. 1–14), Ferguson 2016, then Golub & Pereyra 2003. Take the reduced objective from Ferguson & Quinn 1994 eq. 20: Ferguson 2016's eq. 14 is misprinted. [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) section 6.3 lists these caveats.

## Video walkthrough

[colsto, *I discovered a sound that leaks your exact location (and it's everywhere)*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UytYrhYLOKI), published 2026-11-02, 29 min 12 s. The local annotated transcript is `$DOPPLERTRACK_DATA/reference/youtube/UytYrhYLOKI/transcript.md`.

It documents the national candidate search, manual event-fit bounds, the silence filter, emission-time Doppler modeling, locked two-event scoring, and a separate single-flight search using a hand-traced ridge. The transcript distinguishes narration from on-screen values and editorial checks; the full audio was compared with an independent Whisper pass, and sampled frames and selected full-resolution stills were reviewed. Ambiguous speech and source discrepancies remain marked. In particular, the two displayed winning coordinates differ by about 610 m, and the one-flight example supplies a nominal time in narration but a one-hour window in its comparison slide.

The source recording linked in the description, [4CxVxuMVkMc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4CxVxuMVkMc), is already archived as `raw/audio/youtube/oyster_bay.webm` and `.m4a`; no duplicate was downloaded. It is our Oyster Bay **development** recording, not independent evaluation evidence. The walkthrough's thresholds and scores describe that demonstration and should not be substituted for this fork's measured results or current defaults.

## What's here

### Acoustic Doppler flight-parameter estimation (Phase 2: event fits)

Most of these come from Brian Ferguson's group at the Australian Defence Science and Technology Organisation (DSTO). They are the closest prior work to dopplertrack's `fit`: one ground microphone, the propeller blade-rate tone, and a model of how its observed frequency changes during a pass.

| File | Source | What it is | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ferguson-1992-narrowband-altitude-speed` | Ferguson, *JASA* 92:1403 (1992), 5 pp. | The original method: track the blade-rate tone and least-squares fit a Doppler model with altitude and speed as parameters. Tested on 18 controlled overhead passes; RMS error about 100 ft in altitude (250–1500 ft) and 6 kn in speed. The source frequency was known (68 Hz) and the CPA time read off the plot, so only two parameters were fitted, Ferguson & Lo 2000 (§I, its refs. 4–5) says these passes flew along a line of 15 microphones whose frequency tracks were time-shifted and averaged, though this paper describes one microphone. | The ancestor of our `fit`, which fits all four parameters from one microphone, so its errors understate what to expect even from a clean, controlled pass. Its eq. 2 adds the along-track wind: the Doppler follows motion through the air, while ADS-B gives ground speed. |
| `ferguson-quinn-1994-stft-wigner-ville-localization` | Ferguson & Quinn, *JASA* 96:821 (1994), 7 pp. | Same 18 passes and array. Compares the short-time Fourier transform with the Wigner–Ville distribution for estimating the tone frequency, then fits speed, altitude, rest frequency and CPA time. Gives the closed-form frequency model (eqs. 12–17), the least-squares objective, and a starting procedure that needs no asymptotes (eq. 21): a change point for t_c, then a coarse search over s up to s_max (10 s here). | The reference formulation of our fit objective. A direct comparison point for the fit objectives in Phase 2. Its starting procedure suits truncated passes better than the asymptote one, once s_max is raised to about 150 s for our ranges. |
| `quinn-1995-doppler-speed-range-frequency-amplitude` | Quinn, *JASA* 98:2560 (1995), 7 pp. | Improves the 1994 algorithm by using **amplitude** estimates as weights (each frequency estimate's error variance falls as 1/amplitude²) and derives the estimators' covariance. Claims to work when only part of the Doppler track is available, but its only simulation has CPA mid-track (5 m/s at 500 m), and on raw estimates the fit failed outright with FTI(1) and overestimated the 500 m range by 34 % with FTI(2) (669 ± 99 m); after a 3-point median filter all three estimators came within 4 % (480–512 m, Table I). | Weights the frequency track by reliability, and median-filters it before fitting. Amplitude is a weight here, *not* a range measurement. Partial passes, which real recordings often contain, are still untested. |
| `ferguson-lo-2000-turboprop-rotary-wing` | Ferguson & Lo, *JASA* 108:1763 (2000), 9 pp. | One framework for a narrow-band method (Doppler of the tone at one sensor) and a broadband method (time differences across a sensor array), both tested on the same transits of a turboprop and two helicopter types. | The group's only single-microphone error figures: 7.5 kn and 85 ft for the turboprop, 8.9 kn and 54 ft for the helicopters, with about 12 % of altitude estimates more than 20 % off. Also when tone-based and broadband measurements complement each other. |
| `ferguson-2016-nls-aeroacoustic-source-parameters` | Ferguson, *IET Radar Sonar Navig.* 10:1552 (2016), 9 pp. | Reviews nonlinear least squares (NLS) for three cases: a turboprop from one microphone's frequency track, a jet from multipath delays, and a ground vehicle from array time differences. Finds simpler conventional methods comparable, and good as NLS starting points, though the comparison is about six passes read by eye. | **Read first.** The newest treatment of exactly our fit, with conventional (asymptote and peak-slope) estimates as starting values. Helps decide which Phase 2 estimator to implement. Its printed eqs. 14 and 40 are wrong (eq. 14 squares each term inside the sum; eq. 40 squares a term Ferguson & Lo 2000 eq. 20 doesn't); use Ferguson & Quinn 1994 eq. 20 instead. Its §5 cites Lo & Ferguson 2010, which locates sensors from a moving source (see "Worth considering later"). |

| `timlelt-2017-closed-form-doppler-motion` | Timlelt, Remram & Belouchrani, *Digital Signal Processing* 63:35 (2017), 27 pp. (accepted manuscript) | A **closed-form**, non-iterative solution for speed, altitude, source frequency and CPA time from the instantaneous frequency, for propeller aircraft and helicopters. | An experimental comparator only. ### Frequency tracking (Phase 2)

| File | Source | What it is | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| `iatsenko-2016-ridge-instantaneous-frequency` | Iatsenko, McClintock & Stefanovska, *Signal Processing* 125:290 (2016), 15 pp. (author's open copy) | Extracting a tone's frequency curve from a time-frequency map: an adaptive method based on dynamic path optimization, compared with alternatives on simulated and real data. Code is freely available. | A concrete alternative to compare with our tracker for faint tones. Warns that short-lived signals need stopping rules so the tracker doesn't join unrelated tones, and offers none. Where two components cross, it can follow either unless they differ strongly in amplitude or slope (§6), which is the jet-crossing-prop case. Harmonics aren't covered. Its adaptive scheme penalises slopes far from the median, which on a pass is the steep stretch near CPA. |
| `quinn-hannan-2001-estimation-and-tracking-of-frequency` | Quinn & Hannan, *The Estimation and Tracking of Frequency* (Cambridge, 2001), 282 pp.; book page = PDF page − 12 | The statistics of frequency estimation: the periodogram maximiser and its Cramér–Rao bound (ch. 3), the three-Fourier-coefficient interpolators that `freqtrack` uses (§6.2.2), and tracking a slowly varying frequency at low SNR as a hidden Markov model with the Viterbi and forward–backward recursions (ch. 7, with a real sonar example in §7.5). Also the Doppler pass fit with amplitude-squared weights (§1.4), a joint estimator of a harmonic set's fundamental in which harmonic j adds information as j² (eq. 3.11, Theorem 8), tone-detection thresholds (Fisher's g, §1.3; eq. 3.17), and the finding that under coloured noise the threshold is set by the strongest noise peak in the band searched (§3.3, Tables 3.1–3.2). | Phase 2 item 3's next step is hysteresis in states; ch. 7 is that model, with the transition and emission structure written out, and §7.4 is the HMM tracker to compare the off-state Viterbi against. The two differ in kind: the book's HMM uses complex Fourier coefficients with phase linked across contiguous, unwindowed blocks, which §7.5 finds supplies most of the gain, and estimates the noise variance by EM. The track-lost state is Streit & Barrett 1990's (cited in §7.4), and the book warns it is very sensitive to its transition probabilities. |
| `harris-1978-windows-dft` | Harris, *Proc. IEEE* 66:51 (1978), 33 pp. | The catalogue of DFT windows and their figures of merit; §IV.C defines the overlap correlation c(r) between successive transforms (eq. 17, Table I) and gives Welch's variance of an average of overlapped transforms (eq. 19). | Our STFT frames overlap 87.8 % (2.048 s Hann, 0.25 s hop), so neighbouring frames are not independent samples: by eq. 17 a Hann frame correlates 0.91, 0.67, 0.40 and 0.18 with the next four, and 1 + 2Σc² = 3.9, so a covariance that counts frames as independent (Quinn 1995) is about four times too small, and a 1 s or 0.5 s window at the same hop brings the factor to 1.9 and 1.06. |

### Estimation and detection theory (Phases 2, 4 and detection)

| File | Source | What it is | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kay-1993-ssp-vol1-estimation-theory` | Kay, *Fundamentals of Statistical Signal Processing, Vol. I: Estimation Theory* (Prentice Hall, 1993), 603 pp. | The standard textbook on parameter estimation: Cramér–Rao lower bound (ch. 3), maximum likelihood (ch. 7), least squares (ch. 8), and many worked frequency-estimation examples. Ex. 6.3 locates an emitter by time differences of arrival at antennas whose positions are known, with the unknown emission time removed by differencing: the mirror of our problem (known emitters, unknown receiver), with that time playing our unknown recording clock. | The theory behind turning "how fast the fit score falls off around its optimum" into real uncertainty ranges for each fitted event (gap E1), once the score is a likelihood. Ex. 7.16 and §7.9 show how to get there: a periodogram divided by the local noise level is a tone's log-likelihood with amplitude and phase maximised out, which supports building a curve score in linear power over noise-normalised cells as a likelihood. Today's score, a mean of median-whitened dB, is not one, and a new one still has to settle the sweep within a frame, coloured and correlated noise, overlapping frames, and a per-frame amplitude and phase. Start with ch. 3 and ch. 7; §8.9 is nonlinear least squares, with the separable case (the basis of variable projection) in Ex. 8.10. Eq. 3.30 carries per-event covariances into position. |
| `kay-1998-ssp-vol2-detection-theory` | Kay, *Fundamentals of Statistical Signal Processing, Vol. II: Detection Theory* (Prentice Hall, 1998), 573 pp. | Detection: Neyman–Pearson, matched filters, detecting signals with unknown parameters (composite hypothesis testing), false-alarm control. | The principled basis for `detect`'s score floor and "no flyovers found" result (gap E4), and for the usability check (gap S6): ch. 3–4 and 6–7. Also: ch. 8 (Ex. 8.7, a harmonic-comb detector with an unknown fundamental, whose unpenalised maximum picks a multiple of the true period); ch. 9 (CFAR statistics for unknown noise, §9.3, and Ex. 9.4, why χ² thresholds fail for frequency); ch. 12 (when a tone starts and stops); and §6.8 with App. 6F, the penalised rule for choosing among composite hypotheses, which is the location decision. |
| `golub-pereyra-2003-variable-projection` | Golub & Pereyra, *Inverse Problems* 19:R1 (2003). This copy is the authors' 30-page preprint of July 2002, which numbers equations as section.count, e.g. (12.3). | Survey of variable projection: when a model is linear in some parameters, solve those exactly by linear least squares and optimize only the rest. Covers the theory, algorithms and applications, including Abel 1994's GPS position with an unknown clock bias (§13). | **Read first.** In Ferguson & Quinn's model (eq. 16, f = α + β·z(t_c, s)), α and β enter linearly and carry the rest frequency and speed. Variable projection solves them exactly at each (t_c, s), leaving only timing and geometry to optimize. Its multiple-right-hand-side form (§4) fits the joint problem: position and clock shared, each event's f0 separate. A partial track far from CPA makes α and β nearly collinear; use the SVD form (§2). |
| `chan-towers-1992-doppler-passive-localization` | Chan & Towers, *IEEE TSP* 40:2594 (1992), 5-page issue extract (the paper starts on PDF page 1, after the end of another paper) | Locates a tone source from Doppler-shifted frequencies at five or more known positions. Introducing intermediate variables cuts a 5-D search to a 2-D position grid with a small linear solve at each point. Gives the Cramér–Rao bound. Bounds speed and f0 physically (eqs. 13–14; eq. 14's inequalities look reversed as printed), keeps the 5–6 lowest-cost points because of ghosts, spaces the grid no finer than the bound and refines by Levenberg–Marquardt. Its 2-D grid-plus-linear-solve trick mirrors our `search` over candidate positions: there the linear unknowns would be each event's f0. |
| `white-1982-misspecified-mle` | White, *Econometrica* 50:1 (1982), 26 pp. | Maximum likelihood when the model is wrong: the estimate still converges to the best-fitting wrong model, standard tests become invalid, and a corrected ("sandwich") covariance applies. Three tests of whether the model is wrong: information matrix (eq. 4.1), Hausman (eq. 5.1) and gradient (eq. 5.2). | Separates fit uncertainty from error caused by a wrong motion model (e.g. a turning or climbing aircraft); the tests could flag such an event before it enters a location. The sandwich extends to any smooth sum-of-terms objective, not only likelihoods, but assumes independent observations, which overlapping frames are not. Read when designing uncertainty estimates, after the fitting baseline exists. |
| `raue-2009-profile-likelihood-identifiability` | Raue et al., *Bioinformatics* 25:1923 (2009), 7 pp. (open access) | Profile likelihood: fix one parameter, re-optimize the others, and read confidence intervals off the resulting curve. Tells "structural" non-identifiability (parameters that trade off exactly) apart from "practical" (data too weak to pin them down). Written for biology models, but the method is general. Hessian intervals come out too narrow where the data are weak (Table 1). | The right way to derive each event's speed, distance and time ranges in Phase 2 (gap E1), and to see how they trade off against each other. A profile open on one side still bounds the other, so it limits that parameter one way rather than condemning the fit. Over position, the profile region's shape gives the verdict: one closed piece, several pieces, or a region running to the box edge. |

### Undoing Doppler in the waveform, and combining evidence (Phase 2 items 4–7, Phase 3)

Collected 2026-11-04 on an outside reviewer's advice (the review and its experiment design are summarised in PLAN.md Phase 2 item 8 and section 6.3); Howell 1986 and Ramseier & Pieren 2024 are paywalled and were supplied by the user.

| File | Source | What it is | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| `howell-1986-de-dopplerization-flyover` | Howell, Bradley, McCormick & Brown (Rolls-Royce), *J. Sound Vib.* 105:151 (1986), 17 pp.; supplied by the user | The original single-microphone de-Dopplerisation: signals emitted at equal intervals reach a fixed microphone at the irregular times of eq. 1, so sample the recording at those reception times to rebuild the emission-time history (§2.1). Their simulation (§3.1, Fig. 4) sets the interpolation rule: nearest-sample resampling gives a 10 dB signal-to-noise floor at equal rates, linear interpolation with the recording sampled at twice the analysis rate gives about 35 dB. On an Avro Shackleton (four Griffons with counter-rotating propellers) the corrected 90° spectrum resolves half-engine orders every 20 Hz up to 980 Hz and blade-passing harmonics (53.1 Hz fundamental) to the 17th, which the raw spectrum blurs (§3.3, Figs. 9 and 11). | The propeller demonstration of the warp, with the number PLAN item 8 needs for its interpolator (sample at twice the analysis rate, interpolate linearly, or use band-limited interpolation); their two caveats are ours: the corrected history is right only for the assumed emission point, and reflections act as apparent sources that warp differently. The trajectory came from cameras, and the noise-floor figures are for 1200–1800 Hz noise at a 5 kHz analysis rate; our 40–450 Hz tones at 44.1–48 kHz are far more oversampled. |
| `kelly-wilson-1993-de-dopplerization-aiaa` | Kelly & Wilson, AIAA 93-0737 (1993), 12 pp.; the NTRS record 19930040830 is this paper and has no download | De-Dopplerization of a single microphone's recording from a tracked aircraft position history: for a uniform grid of emission times τ, compute the reception time from the range (their eq. 12), interpolate the measured pressure there (linear), scale by range and the Doppler factor (eq. 30), and Fourier-transform in τ (eq. 31). On an XV-15 flyover at 250 ft the corrected spectrum shows the blade-passing fundamental near 26 Hz and its overtones at 52, 78, 104 and 130 Hz where the raw spectrum smears them, and the 4th and 5th harmonics that smearing erased reappear (§V, Figs. 6–8). | The simplest published form of the warp PLAN item 8 proposes: resample the waveform onto emission time for a candidate trajectory and observer, then look for a stationary line. Their limitations list (§IV: quasi-stationary source, no absorption, uniform atmosphere, ground impedance) is ours too; their overhead case is 250 ft, ours 2–9 km. |
| `gao-2021-doppler-warping` | Gao, Gao, Chi, Wang & Song, *Acta Physica Sinica* 70:124302 (2021), 10 pp., Chinese with an English abstract; open access | The same resampling turned into an estimator ("Doppler-warping"): the warping operator h_D(t) = t + √(r₀² + v²(t − t₀)²)/c (eq. 6) linearises the phase of a tone from a straight constant-speed pass (eq. 7); for each candidate speed, resample, take the spectrum, and minimise its Shannon entropy over the line's band (eq. 9). On a 150 hp fishing boat at 174 m with a 124.5 Hz line, the entropy estimate gives 4.7 m/s against GPS 4.5 and a frequency-fit comparator 5.2 (§5). The "entropy" is of an unnormalised spectrum over a band chosen by eye, the result is one pass, and the comparator was handed f0, r₀ and t₀ (eq. 10). | Spectral concentration as the score for a motion hypothesis instead of brightness along a predicted curve. One hydrophone, one line, r₀ and t₀ assumed known and only speed estimated: whether it separates candidate *locations and flights* on aircraft passes is what item 8 tests. The entropy over a band is also a natural "is this a tone" statistic for item 6. |
| `axelrad-2011-collective-detection` | Axelrad, Bradley, Donna, Mitchell & Mohiuddin, *Navigation* 58:305 (2011), 17 pp. | Collective detection for GNSS: instead of acquiring each satellite alone, map every point of a receiver position-and-clock grid to each satellite's delay and Doppler, add the satellites' correlation powers non-coherently there, and search the grid coarse to fine (Table 1: 1 km, 100 m, 30 m steps). Four weak satellites combined detect at 36.2 dB-Hz, where acquiring the four one by one needs 41.9 (Fig. 4); the "noise-only" tail is fatter than the simple model says because of cross-correlations (§Combining Satellites). A coarse cell is scored by the correlation averaged over the span it covers, so the grid can't step over a narrow peak; the answer is the centroid of the points above a threshold set below the peak, which beat the peak itself (Fig. 13); a second peak is named as an integrity flag. | The closest analogue in the folder to the whole task (known emitters, unknown receiver and clock), and the pattern Phase 3 proposes for several uncertain events: one location and clock, each event's evidence mapped into that space and summed, with an unmatched alternative. Their satellites have known codes; our aircraft have unknown tones, some are inaudible and several can explain one line, so the sum needs an assignment rule (our addition, not theirs). The coarse-to-fine search, the averaged coarse cells, the centroid and the fat null tail transfer directly. |
| `ramseier-pieren-2023-estimation-of-the-fan-rotational-speed-using-flyover-audio-recordings` | Ramseier & Pieren (Empa), *J. Aircraft* 61:58 (2024, online 2023), 15 pp.; supplied by the user | Fan speed (N1) of turbofans from ground flyover recordings with known trajectories: de-Dopplerise in the time domain by asynchronous resampling with a windowed-sinc (Hamming) kernel (eq. 7, §II.C.2); enhance the spectrogram by subtracting, at each instant, the median over the neighbouring frequencies f ± x (eq. 11); then track the blade-passing frequency and its harmonics together by dynamic programming, each harmonic's power added with weight (N+1−h)/N and a slope bound δ (eq. 12, §II.C.3; eq. 14 is the backtrack, and the printed eq. 12 is garbled, so take its intent), with a cepstral fallback for buzz-saw noise. Tracking covers −10 to +2 s around CPA in a band set by eye per aircraft type. Their geometry is known and their aircraft are turbofans at 44.1 kHz; their distance limit is a warning for our 2–9 km passes. |
| `merino-martinez-2019-fan-speed-flyover-ica` | Merino-Martínez, Snellen & Simons (TU Delft), ICA 2019, 8 pp. (open, DEGA) | Fan rotational speed of landing and departing turbofans from flyover recordings, to replace the default thrust in noise-contour models: the blade-passing frequency and its harmonics are tracked in the ordinary spectrogram inside a band predicted from the trajectory (air-traffic-control radar at 4 s, interpolated linearly, not ADS-B) and the Doppler relation (eq. 3), then a least-squares curve fit gives the emitted frequency, allowing small bumps for pilot throttle changes. No de-Dopplerisation, and no ground truth for the fan speeds. | The simpler, curve-in-the-spectrogram approach to the same problem Ramseier & Pieren later solved by warping; what tracking a predicted curve looks like on known-geometry turbofans, though unvalidated, and a reminder that the emitted tone drifts with throttle. |
| `fatela-2024-adsb-flyover-daga` | Fatela, Masullo, Vorländer & Maffei, DAGA 2024, 4 pp. (open, DEGA) | An open ADS-B-plus-microphone pipeline for engine-speed estimation: shift each sample by minus the propagation delay from the ADS-B position (straight path, or ray-traced through a moving inhomogeneous atmosphere), resample evenly, STFT, then a 2-D convolution that favours long narrow tones over short broadband events before tracking the fan tone. | An independent description of the warp with the two ingredients ours would need (delay from ADS-B, even resampling) and the limits it expects, though it reports no validation and tested neither: the method is likely not suited to fast engine-speed changes, and reflected or diffracted paths (urban recordings) may shift the tone differently from the direct path. ## Notes for the plan

From reading these alongside PLAN.md (partly Astra's review):

- **Phase 2.** Call score-drop ranges "fit sensitivity ranges" until they are calibrated. The current brightness/contrast score is not a likelihood, so the Cramér–Rao and profile-likelihood machinery applies only once the objective is turned into one (e.g. a noise model for the frequency track, as in Ferguson & Quinn's least-squares fit). Kay I Ex. 7.16 suggests another route, a new scorer to build and test rather than a reading of today's: brightness in linear power, each cell divided by its local noise level, summed along the curve, with frame overlap accounted for.
- **Phase 2.** The DSTO papers fit frequency only. Quinn 1995 uses amplitude to weight the frequency estimates, not to constrain distance. Lo & Ferguson 2003 (deferred) adds the ground-reflection delay, which does constrain distance, but for broadband sources over hard ground. Distance is the weakest fitted parameter (PLAN section 6.1).
- **Phase 4.** No paper here validates a "located" / "ambiguous" / "insufficient evidence" rule for this task. Kay II gives two separate pieces: searching more hypotheses gives noise more chances to win (eqs. 7.36–7.38, for independent cells, which ours are not), and a hypothesis with more free parameters wins unless penalised (§6.8, Ex. 8.7). So the winner is tested after the whole search against null cases, including a real flyover matched to the wrong flight, since χ² thresholds fail for position (Ex. 9.4).
- **Benchmark.** Hold out whole recordings or days, keeping their clips and augmented copies together. Cawley & Talbot treat independent cases only; this is our extension to grouped recordings, and Dunn, Wasserman & Ramdas 2018 (below) is the conformal version.
- **Labels.** Treat ADS-B passes as candidate events, not confirmed audible flyovers. Hand-check a small subset before trusting detection precision and recall.

## Conversion

```bash
pdf-to-md "$DOPPLERTRACK_DATA/reference/pdf/" -o "$DOPPLERTRACK_DATA/reference/md" --backend mistral --paginate
python3 tools/reference/tidy_latex.py "$DOPPLERTRACK_DATA"/reference/md/*.md
python3 tools/reference/add_labels.py
```

Mistral OCR was chosen over the OpenAI backend because, on sample pages, OpenAI silently altered math (dropped a factor of ½ and a prime) while Mistral didn't.

Two fixes are applied to every file after conversion. Both are safe to re-run, and must be re-run after re-converting a file with `--overwrite`.

- Known conversion errors, reported by an outside reviewer on 2026-11-04 and to be checked against the PDF before use: the Kelly & Wilson conversion loses a minus sign in the time equations (eqs. 8 and 12), and the Ramseier & Pieren conversion mislabels the left-hand side of its eq. 9. Take time equations from the PDF pages.
- `tools/reference/tidy_latex.py` rewrites spaced-out LaTeX (`\mathbf { x }` → `\mathbf{x}`). It changes only whitespace that TeX ignores, and checks every formula still tokenizes identically.
- `tools/reference/add_labels.py` restores 23 equation numbers Mistral dropped: 9 in Kay I, 10 in Kay II and 4 in Angelopoulos & Bates. Every file's printed equation numbers were compared with the Markdown (OCR of each page plus a numbering-gap check), and each restored number was placed by eye against the PDF page. A whole equation gets `\tag{n}`; one row of a multi-row equation gets `\qquad\text{(n)}`.

Mistral also writes some numbers as `\quad (n)` inside the math or as plain text beside the equation (White puts them on the left, e.g. `(5.2) $$…$$`). When looking for an equation, search for both `\tag{4.16}` and `(4.16)`.

Limitations:
- Multi-row derivations are `\begin{array}` blocks. Check alignment against the PDF.
- Figures are images with captions. Plots are not converted to data.
- Empty Markdown pages are blank in the PDF too.
